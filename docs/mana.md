# **Mana**

<p class="mana-tagline"><em>Mana is life.</em></p>

It's in a newborn's first breath, in the slow pulse under an old man's skin, in tree roots buried under winter soil. Most people never feel it, never see it, never think twice about the mana that kept their heart beating.

Maybe one in twenty finds out, usually somewhere around adulthood, that each breath leaves something behind. In them it gathers instead of passing through, at first it's barely something, warmth under the skin, strength that comes too easily, a moment where the world seems to slow down for no reason. Then, if they're lucky, someone teaches them what it means.

Training lets the body hold more, move it faster, waste less on the same motion. Talent helps, discipline helps more. There are supposed limits to all of this, though enough people have broken them over the years that nobody seriously calls a limit absolute anymore.

Past all of that are the ones who make ordinary strength look like a joke. Warriors who lift what should take a team of oxen. Men who can hear a whisper from across a crowded courtyard. Swordsmen who cross a room and leave ten bodies on the floor before the first one lands. The strongest have torn through stone and timber bare-handed. There are old accounts of single warrior leaving behind enough wreckage that people assumed an army had come through.

That kind of power doesn't go unnoticed, neither does the mana itself. Spend it and it leaves traces of an invisible residue that lingers where violence happened, carrying something of whoever left it there. No two are quite the same, like a fingerprint pressed into the world.

For most people, mana is just the line between being alive and being dead.

For a few, it's the line between what they were born with and what they could become.

## **Mana Reinforcement and Resistance**

As a Praedari becomes more experienced, mana does more than provide power for deliberate techniques. It begins to settle deeper into the body itself.

A properly trained Praedari develops a natural resistance to attacks carrying **no magical property**.

Mana reinforces flesh, bone, organs, and the structures beneath them. Steel still cuts. Bullets still strike. Fire still burns. But considerably more force is required to produce the same injury that an ordinary person would suffer.

A blade that should cut deeply may leave only a shallow wound. A bullet may fail to penetrate properly. An impact capable of breaking an ordinary man's bones may leave a trained Praedari bruised rather than crippled.

They are not invulnerable. Sufficient mundane force can still overwhelm this reinforcement.

That begins to change among **Exceptional Praedari**.

At that level, mana is no longer merely strengthening the body.

The concentration of mana within them becomes so great that it develops a kind of **density of its own**, permeating the body and resisting anything attempting to pass through it. Flesh remains flesh, but reaching that flesh becomes another matter entirely.

Against such a Praedari, an ordinary weapon may strike perfectly and still fail to produce a meaningful wound.

Bullets flatten or stop. Blades fail to bite deeply enough. Shrapnel, arrows, and ordinary weapons may still throw the body, tear clothing, or leave superficial marks, but they cannot reliably overcome the density of mana protecting what lies beneath.

For an attack to properly wound an Exceptional Praedari, **a magical property is mandatory**.

The attack must carry mana of its own.

Even then, merely possessing magic does not guarantee a serious injury. The mana behind the attack must possess enough power to contest the mana protecting the target. Weak enchantments may penetrate where mundane weapons cannot, yet still accomplish little against someone whose mana vastly exceeds their own.

The greater the Praedari, the more severe this difference becomes.

To reliably injure them, the attacking force must increasingly approach their own level of mana.

Against the strongest Praedari, this creates a brutal reality:

**power must be answered with power.**

An ordinary soldier may fire directly into one and accomplish almost nothing. Give that same soldier a properly enchanted weapon and the attack can finally draw blood. Give him a weapon carrying mana strong enough to rival the target, and the wound becomes something the Praedari can no longer simply disregard.

This is why magical weapons become a necessity rather than a luxury when fighting the highest ranks of Praedari.

Against ordinary people, enchantment makes a weapon better.

Against an Exceptional Praedari, enchantment is what makes the weapon a weapon at all.

## **Praedari**

Praedari is the name given to anyone born able to hold onto more mana than the body actually needs beyond just staying alive. For most, the gift doesn't show itself until somewhere around adulthood, and from there, training decides what that surplus becomes. It might sharpen the mind, strengthen flesh and bone, quicken the nerves, deepen the senses, make minds outstretched beyond what is normal, or get shaped into outright magic. The path looks different for every Praedari, but the advantage is there from the start.

Even a Praedari who's considered weak by the standards of their own kind is rarely weak in any ordinary sense. Someone written off as mediocre can still, with proper training, end up stronger, faster, tougher, or sharper-eyed than an average person could ever hope to match. The gap only widens as talent and discipline pile up. A single Praedari can be powerful enough to decide the outcome of a battle, alone with a movement of a finger.

Mana changes the body , the stronger a Praedari gets, the longer they tend to live, since a body steeped in mana doesn't age at the same rate as an ordinary one. Wounds close more readily, organs hold up longer, and years that would wear another person down seem to pass more gently over them. Among the truly powerful, a lifespan can stretch well beyond anything most people would call natural.

That's why Praedari tend to be respected and feared in roughly equal measure. Their gifts have produced famous warriors, scholars, artists, rulers, hunters, and spellcasters over the years, but admiration for them has never crowded out of caution.

## **Praedari Ranks**

Not all Praedari develop to the same degree.

Valeria formally divides them into six ranks according to **mana capacity, density, control, output, and the extent to which mana has changed the individual**.

These ranks do not measure spellcasting alone.

As mana develops, it changes the Praedari as a whole. The body becomes stronger and faster. Reflexes sharpen. Endurance increases. Senses grow more acute. Thought becomes quicker, memory more reliable, and concentration easier to maintain. Even qualities such as presence and force of personality may become more pronounced.

Mana does not make every Praedari equally gifted in all things. A brilliant Peritus may remain more intelligent than a dull Potens, just as natural talent, education, temperament, and experience continue to matter.

It strengthens what is already there.

Mana development also affects **aging and lifespan**. The stronger a Praedari becomes, the more slowly the body deteriorates. Powerful Praedari may preserve their youth for decades beyond what would be natural for their race, while the greatest among them can live far longer than ordinary people.

The system itself is **Valerian**.

Its standards are considered severe across much of the continent. Centuries of organized magical education and a vast population of trained Praedari have made the Empire unusually demanding. A Praedari regarded as merely competent in Valeria may already be considered elite in another human kingdom.

The ranks are not guarantees of victory. Discipline, experience, equipment, preparation, and individual ability still matter.

The rank describes what a Praedari's mana has become.

### **Tiro**

Tiro is the lowest recognized stage of Praedari development. Almost all Tiro within Valeria are first- or second-year students studying within one of the Four Chartered Academies, though the term can also apply to newly discovered Praedari who have only recently begun formal cultivation.

They are still students.

A Tiro may already possess abilities far beyond those of an ordinary person, but their mana remains immature. Their control is incomplete, their cultivation is still developing, and they have not yet demonstrated the consistency expected from a fully trained Praedari.

Because of this, Valeria does not yet regard a Tiro as an asset of the Empire in the same sense as a graduated Praedari. They are instead considered **future potential**. Their education, performance, discipline, and development are still being observed to determine what that potential will eventually become.

Some Tiro will never progress particularly far.

Others are only beginning careers that may one day place them among the strongest people in the Empire.

At this stage, Valeria does not concern itself too much with which is which.

That is what the academies are meant to discover.

### **Peritus**

Peritus is the first rank considered to possess a firm and reliable foundation in mana.

Most Peritus are graduates of the Imperial academies, though some late-year students may already reach this level before completing their education. By this stage, their mana reserves have grown considerably beyond those of a Tiro, and they have developed enough control to use mana consistently and safely in everyday work.

Most Peritus do not become soldiers.

They enter ordinary civilian professions throughout the Empire. They work in agriculture, construction, mechanics, metallurgy, transportation, research, medicine, manufacturing, and countless other trades where even basic mana reinforcement and control can make a worker considerably more capable than someone without it.

In this sense, Peritus form much of the practical foundation of Valeria's mana-using population. They are not extraordinary by Imperial standards. They are trained professionals who happen to possess abilities that would be considered exceptional in many places beyond Valeria.

Those who enter the military are treated differently.

Military Peritus are generally assigned to dedicated **Praedari units**, where their abilities can be properly supported and coordinated. These units are equipped with specialized weapons, armor, and magical items designed to take advantage of the soldiers' mana and allow them to perform well beyond the capabilities of ordinary troops.

For most academy graduates, Peritus is where education ends and actual life begins.

### **Potens**

Potens represents an experienced Praedari, someone who has moved beyond the foundations of mana and learned to use it with practiced confidence.

By this stage, their mana reserves have grown considerably beyond those of a Peritus, while their control has become far more refined. They no longer simply understand how to use mana correctly. Years of repetition, work, training, and experience have made its use instinctive.

Most Potens are veterans in one form or another.

Many still work ordinary civilian professions throughout the Empire. A Potens may be an experienced mechanic, a respected agricultural worker, a skilled metallurgist, a researcher, an engineer, a physician, or a blacksmith whose craft has been refined through decades of working with mana. They are often the people others turn to when something difficult needs to be done properly.

A Peritus has the foundation.

A Potens has experience.

Within the military, Potens mark the beginning of what Valeria formally considers its **Veteran Praedari units**. Soldiers who reach this rank possess enough mana, control, and practical experience to be trusted with more demanding assignments and more advanced magical equipment than ordinary Praedari formations.

They are not merely trained mana-users anymore.

They are veteran practitioners, Praedari who have used their abilities long enough that mana has become inseparable from the way they work, fight, and solve problems.

### **Excelsus**

Excelsus represents one of the most noticeable leaps in Praedari development.

The difference between a Potens and an Excelsus is not merely a larger mana reserve. By this stage, the Praedari's capacity has increased substantially, but more importantly, their control has become extraordinarily refined. Mana is no longer something they simply use well. It is something they can direct with a degree of precision, efficiency, and focus that most Praedari will never achieve.

This is where many begin to encounter the limits of their own development.

A great number of Praedari will remain Potens for the rest of their lives. Reaching Excelsus usually requires years of deliberate cultivation beyond ordinary experience, even for talented individuals. Those who succeed are uncommon enough that they rarely remain anonymous.

Excelsus are often renowned within their profession or region.

An Excelsus working in agriculture may oversee projects affecting entire provinces. A researcher may become known throughout Imperial academic circles. An engineer, metallurgist, physician, or artificer may be entrusted with work too valuable or difficult to place in ordinary hands. Whatever profession they follow, the Empire tends to use them carefully.

There simply are not enough of them to waste.

Their control has also developed beyond the limits normally seen among Peritus and Potens. An Excelsus can extend their mana beyond their own body with deliberate precision, shaping and directing it without relying entirely upon physical contact or magical items.

This refinement is also what makes Excelsus particularly dangerous.

Their mana has reached the point where it can overcome the protection of the ranks above them. An Excelsus possesses enough capacity and sufficient control to **truly wound an Eminens**, and even a Supremus can be injured by an attack carrying mana of this level.

That does not make the ranks equal.

An Eminens or Supremus may still possess vastly greater reserves, durability, and overall power. Excelsus simply marks the point where the Praedari has crossed the threshold necessary to meaningfully harm them.

Around **half of all Excelsus in Valeria serve within the military**.

Within the Imperial military, they form the core of the **Elite Praedari Units**. Potens may serve among veteran Praedari formations, but Excelsus are reserved for situations where ordinary or veteran forces are no longer considered sufficient.

Their numbers are limited enough that commanders cannot afford to deploy them carelessly. They are concentrated where their abilities can have the greatest effect and committed when the situation genuinely warrants their presence.

They are not soldiers the Empire spends casually.

### **Eminens**

Eminens represents another enormous leap in Praedari development.

An Eminens possesses vastly greater mana reserves than an Excelsus, but the defining difference is the **density** that mana has achieved. Their mana has become so abundant and so tightly controlled that it saturates the body completely and begins to emanate beyond it naturally.

This is not a failure of control.

There is simply so much mana concentrated within them that it can no longer remain entirely contained.

That density fundamentally changes how difficult they are to injure.

Non-magical weapons can still strike an Eminens. Blades may cut them. Bullets may hit. Explosions may throw them from their feet. But without mana behind the attack, the resulting wounds are generally superficial. What would tear through an ordinary person may leave little more than a shallow injury upon an Eminens.

To inflict a truly serious wound requires mana powerful enough to penetrate that density.

**Excelsus is the minimum rank considered capable of reliably doing so.**

Reaching Eminens naturally is extraordinarily rare. A handful of exceptional Praedari manage it through their own talent, cultivation, and experience, but they are unusual even among Eminens.

The overwhelming majority reach this rank through the direct assistance of the Empire.

Excelsus who display exceptional potential, and occasionally extraordinary Potens whose development attracts Imperial attention, may be offered entry into further mana cultivation programs funded by the government. The Empire provides the instruction, resources, equipment, training, and time necessary to push their development beyond what most Praedari could reasonably achieve alone.

The investment is enormous.

So is the result.

For this reason, approximately **ninety-five percent of Eminens serve within the Imperial military**.

They stand beyond what the military normally calls elite. An Excelsus may belong to an Elite Praedari Unit. An Eminens is something far rarer, a warrior whose presence can change what is possible on a battlefield.

Not every campaign has an Eminens assigned to it.

Even major formations may operate without one.

The Empire does not deploy them merely because a battle is difficult. Their numbers are too small, their development too costly, and their value too great. When Eminens are committed, they are sent where the Empire has determined that overwhelming Praedari strength is genuinely necessary.

Their equipment reflects the same philosophy.

A military Eminens is typically equipped in **Praestans-grade equipment from head to toe at the minimum**, with weapons, armor, and supporting magical items chosen specifically for an individual capable of sustaining and exploiting equipment of that level.

They are not simply stronger soldiers.

They are among the most heavily cultivated, extensively equipped, and carefully deployed military assets the Empire possesses.

When an Eminens enters the field, the battle has already become serious enough for Valeria to spend something it does not possess in abundance.

### **Supremus**

Supremus is the highest recognized rank of Praedari within Valeria.

There is no rank beyond it.

At this stage, mana capacity, density, control, and output have reached extraordinary levels even compared with an Eminens. The difference is severe enough that a single Supremus can become a military force in their own right. Against ordinary soldiers, even in considerable numbers, they are devastating. Some are capable of breaking small armies without requiring another formation beside them.

They are exceedingly rare, and almost none live ordinary lives.

Most Supremus within Valeria are tied directly to the Imperial military, the Imperial Family, or institutions serving the Throne. Those who hold military authority are commonly found among the highest commanders, champions, duelists, and strategic assets of the Empire.

A particularly important source of Supremus is the **Custodes Praetoriani**.

The Custodes are created through the sealed **Custodes Program**, where candidates undergo sustained cultivation from an early age. Their bodies, minds, mana, discipline, and understanding of service are developed toward a single purpose: lifelong service to the Throne. The program is not ordinary military training. It exists to produce guardians capable of operating beyond the limits expected even of Valeria's elite Praedari.

Many of Valeria's Supremus therefore emerge from the Custodes or eventually enter their ranks. The Custodes are among the few Imperial institutions specifically structured to cultivate, equip, and employ Praedari of this magnitude.

A Custodian is not simply sent where another elite soldier might suffice. Their order exists to protect the Emperor, recognized heirs, lawful succession, and the continuity of the Imperial Throne itself. Their appearance outside those duties means the situation has already surpassed what ordinary Imperial institutions are expected to handle.

The Imperial Family is likewise counted among the Supremus.

Those Supremus who belong neither to the Custodes nor the Imperial Family are still rarely ordinary citizens. They tend to occupy extremely senior military, governmental, academic, religious, or Imperial positions. At this degree of power, the Empire has little interest in allowing someone to simply disappear into obscurity.

Even within the rank itself, however, there is enormous variation.

Supremus has no upper boundary. Someone who has only recently crossed into the rank and one of the greatest Praedari alive may carry the same formal classification despite a vast difference in power.

An Excelsus possesses enough mana to truly wound one.

An Eminens may be powerful enough to confront one.

But another Supremus is among the few opponents capable of meeting one without beginning the fight at an overwhelming disadvantage.

They are not merely the elite of Valeria.

They are the people Valeria creates, cultivates, and preserves for the situations in which ordinary definitions of military force have stopped being sufficient.
